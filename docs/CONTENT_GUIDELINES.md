# Listenly content guidelines

The learner must be able to trust the recording, the questions, the marking, and the explanation. Generated content becomes usable practice only after these agree and have been reviewed together.

## Keep the practice faithful to its task

Full Listening tests contain four parts and 40 questions. Parts 1 and 2 use everyday social contexts; Parts 3 and 4 use educational or academic contexts. Part 1 is a two-person conversation, Parts 2 and 4 are single-speaker recordings, and Part 3 is a discussion. Use the [official format](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-listening) for authoritative details and the chosen delivery format for timing.

Use original or properly licensed content. Describe Listenly material as IELTS-style practice. Internal labels such as `official` do not establish affiliation or permission to reproduce exam content.

## Review a part as one piece

1. The situation makes sense and the language sounds natural.
2. The final recording matches the approved transcript, including names, numbers, corrections, and speaker order.
3. Each question has enough evidence in the audio for an unambiguous accepted answer.
4. Question order, instructions, word limits, and answer variants agree with the recording and task.
5. Incorrect choices are plausible but can be ruled out from the evidence.
6. The explanation identifies the relevant meaning or instruction; it does not just repeat the correct answer.
7. Review material includes the relevant transcript and enough audio context to understand it.
8. Maps and other visuals are legible and correspond to what is spoken.

Check spelling and legitimate answer variants deliberately. Do not reject a correct form merely because it differs from one stored string, or accept a form that violates the question. Use official marking guidance when uncertain; learner forum replies are evidence of confusion, not marking rules.

## Audio and questions share the same source

Approve the transcript before producing the final audio and questions. Recheck both if the transcript changes. Keep speaker content separate from exam instructions and production settings so one edit does not silently alter another layer.

Use clear, natural speech without making correct answers unnaturally prominent. Do not force a fixed pause around every answer. Give dialogue speakers appropriate turns; label single-speaker content as a talk or lecture. Review the final recording by listening, not only by reading its transcript.

Store reviewed assets and references so they can be reused in full tests and one-part practice. A generated audio file by itself is not a completed exercise. A technically publishable test still needs editorial approval.

## Make review worth the learner's time

Show the learner's answer and the accepted answer together. Provide replay of the relevant passage, transcript help, and a brief explanation. Explain a tempting wrong option when useful. Let learners review correct answers they guessed, and report possible mistakes in the material.

Question type describes the task, such as multiple choice. A learning issue describes what happened, such as missing a correction. Do not label a learner with a specific weakness solely from their lowest-scoring part.

## Use the Part 1 reference carefully

The [Part 1 generation reference](references/IELTS_Listening_Part_1_Generation_Specification.docx) contains scenario examples and research-derived production targets. Its preferred dialogue range is 600–760 words and its target pace is about 135–145 words per minute. These are drafting guides, not official IELTS requirements, hard validation rules, or proof of difficulty.

The reference describes a 56-example review, but that underlying analysis was not independently reproduced in this documentation work. Use the reference only for its specialized content purpose. It does not limit the product to Part 1 or require a particular development sequence.

## Publication decision

Publish when the content owner has checked the complete recording, tasks, accepted answers, and review evidence, with no unresolved conflict. Record outstanding content problems before expanding the library. Check actual duration and quality rather than treating word count, AI generation success, or a status badge as approval.
